# 64-bit multiplication helper at `0x00456640`

The 52-byte `__allmul` routine is represented as typed C++ that returns the
low 64 bits of the product of two 64-bit operands. The implementation splits
the low 32-bit product into 16-bit limbs, then adds the two cross-products to
the high word. It does not use a wide multiplication, so the body cannot
recursively call the same compiler runtime helper.

## Ghidra evidence

Live Ghidra identifies `__allmul` as a four-word `__stdcall` function with a
`longlong` return. Its body spans `0x00456640..0x00456673`; both return paths
use `RET 0x10`. The words at `[ESP+4]` and `[ESP+8]` are the low and high
halves of the left operand; `[ESP+0xC]` and `[ESP+0x10]` are the low and high
halves of the right operand. The result is returned in `EDX:EAX`.

Ghidra reports eight call-site references: four in `FUN_0043af30`, two in
`FUN_0043b110`, one in `FUN_00457a7c`, and one at `0x004528F7` in an
ownerless code fragment. The helper has no callees. The call at `0x0043B308`
pushes the right high and low words, then the left high and low words, which
matches the four declared arguments. All call paths rely on the callee to
remove the 16 argument bytes.

Ghidra types the two high words as signed integers. The C++ model uses
unsigned words so the high-half additions have defined modulo-`2^32` behavior,
which matches the helper's low-64-bit result.

## Symbol integration and verification

The semantic entry is `_Allmul_00456640@16`. Existing assembly callers keep
their `_asm_allmul_00456640` reference; the maintained fixed-address linker
script retains that raw alias in the `regions.json` symbol map at
`0x00456640`, alongside the canonical symbol.

The source models the arithmetic and stack ABI; the compiler-generated bytes
have not been checked against retail. No build, probe, or test was run.
