# The immediate-operand copy is the same tie-break — `0x0043CD30` is toolkit-blocked

2026-09-19, wave 11, agent `SchedulerRemove`. Wave 10 settled that retail copies a
call's two register arguments in **ascending parameter-home order** and this
toolkit in **descending home order**, and it listed `0x0043CD30` as the one image
site where the *second* operand is an immediate (`mov %esi,%ecx ; mov $0x492590,%edx
; call 0x43D390`) rather than a second register. This wave asks the narrower
question that listing left open — does the immediate form escape the rule? — and
answers it **no**: the immediate operand obeys the same descending-home tie-break,
on both codegen paths and both c2 revisions on this machine, and no source shape
inside that rule can write the first home first. The region was not modelled beyond
the site and nothing was landed or registered.

## 1. The site

`0x0043CD30` is one 786-byte function (manifest `asm_FUN_0043cd30_0043cd30`,
`src/platform/sound_device_init.cpp`, region `0x0043CD30..0x0043D042`; the
manifest's `asm_FUN_0043cd30_part_0043cf60` is the target of the internal `jmp` at
`0x43CF55`, not a second region). Its last block is the 37-slot WAV preload loop
and its one immediate-operand call setup is in that loop. The declared 786 bytes
were **not** emitted as a model this wave — see §6 — so the measured artifact below
is the site itself, not a whole-region size:

```text
0043cf60:  83 3d b4 77 49 00 02   cmp    DWORD PTR ds:0x4977b4,0x2
0043cf67:  0f 84 …                je     0x43d02a
0043cf6d:  8b 0c b5 40 4b 47 00   mov    ecx,DWORD PTR [esi*4+0x474b40]
0043cf74:  51                     push   ecx
0043cf75:  8b ce                  mov    ecx,esi            <-- arg1 home (ECX)
0043cf77:  ba 90 25 49 00         mov    edx,0x492590       <-- arg2 home (EDX), immediate
0043cf7c:  e8 0f 04 00 00         call   0x43d390
0043cf81:  85 c0                  test   eax,eax
```

The callee `0x43D390` fixes the homes: `mov ebx,edx` / `mov esi,ecx` at
`0x43d3a3`/`0x43d3b2` and `ret 4` at `0x43d63a` make it a three-argument
`__fastcall` — index in **ECX**, device pointer in **EDX**, slot pointer on the
stack. It is still `asm`, so a modelled caller reaches it the way a cluster does:
an opaque `extern "C" __fastcall` declaration bound absolutely at `0x43D390`.

## 2. The experiment that settles it: the immediate is an ordinary copy operand

Two callees with the *same* two-register ABI and the *same* live values, called in
the retail loop shape (37-iteration `for`, state check, table load pushed first).
Plain `/O2` (`/Gz /MT /Gy /G6 /GF /GS /Zl`), `cl 13.10.6030`:

```text
A  f(i, &g_device)          ->  ba 00 00 00 00   mov $imm,%edx   |  8b ce  mov %esi,%ecx   |  call
B  f(&g_device, i)          ->  8b d6            mov %esi,%edx   |  b9 00 00 00 00  mov $imm,%ecx  |  call
(retail A, for comparison)  ->  8b ce            mov %esi,%ecx   |  ba 90 25 49 00  mov $imm,%edx  |  call
```

`A` is the site's shape and `B` is its mirror: the immediate moved from the second
home to the first. The toolkit's **first** emitted copy writes **EDX** in both —
in `B` that is the *register* operand and the immediate goes second. So the copy
order is decided by the **destination home**, not by the operand kind: the first
copy always targets the *second* parameter's home, and swapping the homes only
exchanges the two destination registers (`ba … 8b ce` becomes `8b d6 b9 …`). For
the byte sequence retail has (`8b ce ba …`) the first emitted copy would have to
write **ECX**, which no assignment of operands can produce, because retail's first
copy is a plain `mov %esi,%ecx` into a home the toolkit only ever writes second.

The same mirror at retail's arity (three arguments, slot pushed first) confirms it
on the *native* `/LTCG` path:

```text
/LTCG  f3(i, &g_device, table[i])      ->  ba a0 20 40 00  mov $imm,%edx | 8b ce  mov %esi,%ecx | e8 70 c3 03 00 call
/LTCG  f3(&g_device, i, table[i])      ->  8b d6           mov %esi,%edx | b9 a0 20 40 00 mov $imm,%ecx | e8 70 c3 03 00 call
```

## 3. The one class that *does* write home 1 first — and why the site is not in it

Across the sweeps one family emits home 1 before home 2: when home 1's operand is
*computed* rather than copied. The computation (`mov (%esi),%ecx` load,
`lea (%esi,%edi,1),%ecx`, `movswl %si,%ecx`, `movzwl`) is scheduled ahead of the
immediate; the immediate then follows.

```text
plain /O2  f(i, g_count[i])        (arg1 a memory load)   ->  mov (%esi),%ecx | mov $imm,%edx | call
plain /O2  f(i + j, &g_device)     (arg1 an lea)          ->  lea (%esi,%edi,1),%ecx | mov $imm,%edx | call
plain /O2  f((short)i, &g_device)  (arg1 a conversion)    ->  movswl %si,%ecx | mov $imm,%edx | call
```

Retail's site is not in this family: its home-1 instruction is `8b ce`, a plain
2-byte register copy. `lea ecx,[esi]` would be `8d 0e` and a load would be
`8b 0c b5 …`/`8b 4d xx`; retail's two bytes at `0x43CF75` are `8b ce`, which is the
copy form, so no computed-operand shape can be substituted for it. Every
plain-copy shape emitted the immediate first.

## 4. Setups measured (both codegen paths, both c2 revisions)

* `/tmp/SchedulerRemove/imm.cpp` (3) and `imm2.cpp` (5): the retail loop shape, its
  mirror, a small-literal immediate (`mov $0x2b110,%edx | mov %esi,%ecx`), the
  settled register pair (`mov %ebx,%edx | mov %esi,%ecx`) and the three-argument
  retail arity — **all descending**, all on plain `/O2`.
* `/tmp/SchedulerRemove/sweep.py` (20 shapes, 22 call setups): `for`/`while`/`do`/
  `goto` loops, tail position, `if`/`else`, `switch`, nested loops, two sequential
  calls, an unrolled duo, `short` and parameter-driven counters, register and
  memory operand provenance, a `Device&` reference, a cast, a literal-cast address,
  a local copy of the index, and a conditional immediate. **16 of the 22 setups are
  a plain register copy into home 1 and all 16 emit `mov $imm,%edx | mov %esi,%ecx`**;
  four are the computed-operand family of §3 (`mov (%esi),%ecx` / `lea` / `movswl`
  ahead of the immediate), and the remaining two pass a *literal* as argument 1
  (`mov $0x2,%ecx`) and so are not `(reg,imm)` setups at all.
* `/tmp/SchedulerRemove/press.py` (10 shapes, 10 setups): EDX pressure immediately
  before the call — `imul`, two stores, a `test`/`jle` guard, and an `idiv` whose
  result register *is* EDX — all still `mov $imm,%edx | mov %esi,%ecx`. Readiness
  and pressure do not move the pair.
* `/tmp/SchedulerRemove/press2.py` (2): the retail tail shape with EBX/EBP/EDI
  live across the loop, so the counter lands in ESI and the load scratch competes
  with the argument homes — still `mov $imm,%edx | mov %esi,%ecx`.
* Native `/LTCG` (`/tmp/SchedulerRemove/ltcglab/lab.py`, `/GL` compile + `/LTCG`
  link, opaque callee bound absolutely as a cluster binds it): retail shape,
  retail shape under retail-like pressure, literal immediate, the two- and
  three-argument mirrors — all descending, identical to plain `/O2`.
* **Both c2 revisions**: `cl 13.10.6030` (pinned) and `13.10.3052` (Visual C++
  Toolkit 2003, `c2.dll` `52af3d0…`) emit the same descending pair on the whole
  20-shape sweep, including `ba 00 00 00 00 | 8b ce`.

One escape hatch was tested and is *not* a route to retail's bytes: with the callee
body visible to the `/GL` pass (`__declspec(noinline)` body in the same
translation unit) the link picked a different internal convention altogether —
index in EAX, device pushed, item in ECX, `mov 0x4(%esp),%eax` in the callee — so
the immediate never reaches EDX and the pair the site needs never forms. That is
the "different callee, different ABI" outcome, not the tie-break escaping.

## 5. Retail census

Scanning every adjacent ECX/EDX home-copy pair in `.text` (`census_imm.py`, over an
objdump disassembly of the whole section, classifying each source as reg/mem/imm):

```text
ecxfirst (reg,reg)  52   direct-call-next 40     (the settled Scheduler_Remove pairs)
ecxfirst (reg,imm)   2   direct-call-next  1     <-- 0x43CF75 (the other is `and %edx,%ecx`)
ecxfirst (mem,mem) 220   direct-call-next  1
ecxfirst (mem,reg)  14   direct-call-next  2
ecxfirst (mem,imm)  11   direct-call-next  0
ecxfirst (imm,mem)  27   direct-call-next  0
ecxfirst (reg,mem)   8   direct-call-next  0
edxfirst (reg,reg)   4   direct-call-next  0
edxfirst (mem,reg)  37   direct-call-next 14     (the load-first rule both compilers share)
edxfirst (mem,mem) 163   direct-call-next  2
edxfirst (imm,imm)   5   direct-call-next  0
edxfirst (imm,mem)   1   direct-call-next  0
edxfirst (imm,reg)   1   direct-call-next  0
edxfirst (mem,imm)   5   direct-call-next  0
edxfirst (reg,mem)   1   direct-call-next  0
```

There is exactly **one** `(reg→ECX, imm→EDX)` pair immediately before a direct call
in the image — this site — and no `(imm→ECX, reg→EDX)` pair before a direct call at
all. The immediate form is therefore the whole of `0x0043CD30`'s share of this
tie-break, and it is unreachable for the same reason as the register pair.

## 6. The residual the region would carry, and what was not modelled

The site is 7 bytes wide either way, and the transposition moves every one of them:
retail `8b ce ba 90 25 49 00`, this toolkit `ba 90 25 49 00 8b ce` with the
immediate's real address, so region-relative `+0x245 .. +0x24B` (7 of the region's
786 bytes) all differ. The `call` displacement and everything after it are
unchanged by the swap.

That number is the *site*, not a whole-region measurement: the 786-byte function was
deliberately not modelled (the assignment was the immediate-operand question), and
one further divergence at the same site shows why a whole-region model would be
needed before any byte count could be believed. In every synthetic reproduction of
the loop tail the table load lands in **EAX** (`mov 0x…(,%esi,4),%eax ; push %eax`),
while retail puts it in **ECX** (`mov ecx,[esi*4+0x474b40] ; push ecx`) — a scratch
register choice that survives the retail-like pressure of §4 and is not part of the
tie-break. Until the whole function's allocation is reproduced that choice is
unexplained, so the honest statement is: the site is toolkit-blocked, and whether
the remaining 779 bytes are reachable is unknown and moot while the site is not.

## 7. Disposition

`0x0043CD30` should move from "under investigation" to the toolkit-blocked list next
to `0x00401110`, `0x00405620` and `0x0040D530` (done in
`toolkit_blocked_regions.md` §1 this wave). The disposition lever is unchanged: a
compiler whose first copy for `f(task, sched)` is `mov %ecx,%esi` closes this site
and the sibling set wholesale. No declaration, anchor, root, caller, callee
visibility, pressure ballast or statement order inside this toolkit works: the one
lever that does change the emitted bytes - giving the callee's body to the `/GL`
pass - changes them to a different internal convention, not to retail's (§4).
