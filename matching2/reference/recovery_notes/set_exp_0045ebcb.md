# `__set_exp` semantic model at `0x0045EBCB`

The source body expresses the observed double-word update in ordinary C++
under the canonical `SetExp_0045EBCB` symbol. The shared header declares it
and the typed legacy `_asm_set_exp_0045ebcb` alias. `regions.json` records the
42-byte region as semantic and preserves the former assembly row.

## Ghidra evidence

Ghidra identifies `__set_exp` at `0x0045EBCB`, with body `0x0045EBCB..0x0045EBF4`
(42 bytes), an EBP frame, and a plain `RET`. The body reads a double at
`[EBP+8]` and a full dword at `[EBP+0x10]`. It loads the input double and stores
it to an 8-byte local, takes the old high word from `[EBP+0x0E]`, adds `0x3FE`
to the dword argument, shifts it left four bits, combines it with the old high
word masked by `0x800F`, writes the low 16 bits into the local double's high
word, then reloads and returns that value in the x87 register.

The decompiler reports the second parameter as `short`, but the instruction at
`0x0045EBD0` reads a dword. At the two `__decomp` call sites (`0x0045ED36` and
`0x0045ED49`), the caller reserves three dwords for the double and exponent,
then runs `ADD ESP,0xC`. The call at `0x0045EC2B` likewise pushes the exponent
and two dwords for the double, then cleans 12 bytes. Ghidra does not assign
`0x0045EC2B` to a function, but that address lies in the already-semantic
`0x0045EC0A` region, whose C++ source also declares this helper as
`double __cdecl(double, int)`.

Ghidra lists no direct callees. The C++ model keeps the lower 48 input bits,
rebuilds the high word from the argument and the preserved sign/fraction bits,
and returns the resulting double. The integer conversion and shift are
unsigned so they preserve the x86 dword wraparound used by the body.

## Limits

The original `FLD`/`FSTP` sequence may affect x87 exception/status state for
signaling NaNs. The C++ bit representation models the returned bit pattern, not
those floating-point side effects. No build, probe, or test was run, and
generated bytes have not been compared with retail.
