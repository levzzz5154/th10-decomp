## What the regions are

body is the second-stage selection of `0x00405300`'s shape inlined:

family are `0x004487C0` (`mov eax,0x10`) and `0x00448810` (`mov eax,0x13`); the
neighbouring task `0x00448740` (bucket 0x0E, with the clip words cleared) was

| three `data_00491c10->RenderStage1BucketNN()` calls in the context loop | 356 (measured) | 72 | manager on the stack, `ret $0x4` | byte 0 (`57` vs retail's `56`) |

The measured failure of the first shape is worth recording, because it is the
opposite of the usual "shape the caller" instinct: the body was already exactly
72 bytes and differed only from byte 0 — `push edi / mov edi,0x491E94 / ... ` with
`mov 0x8(%esp),%edi` for the manager and `ret $0x4` at the end, i.e. the pass had
given the task a private *stack* convention because the only caller was the
synthetic context. Rooting the three symbols (`/INCLUDE:`) pins their declared
`__thiscall` convention — ECX receiver, plain `RET` — which is exactly retail's,
and *that* is what makes the body exact; the context call was never what shaped
it. Keeping the anchor out of the driver then removed the last piece of shared
state, so `promote_scratch.py` cannot revert this landing.

## The 0x00448980 anchor

`0x00448980` reads its manager from **EDI** and its bucket index from **EAX**
(`imul eax,index,0x3AC / mov (%eax,%edi),%esi`), and no declaration names those
registers. The anchor is a compiled body at the callee's address carrying only
its argument shape: the bucket's list walk keeps the manager live across the
object hook and the draw dispatch (the pressure that earns EDI), and the index is
read once (which leaves it in EAX). It emitted retail's convention and retail's
exact size:

```text
448980: 69 C0 AC 03 00 00    imul  eax,eax,0x3AC
448986: 56                   push  esi
448987: 8B B4 38 00 DB 72 00 mov   esi,[eax+edi+0x72DB00]   ; manager in EDI, index in EAX
...
4489C1: 5E C3                pop esi / ret                   ; EDI is not preserved: it is the argument
```

Its own bytes are never exported (they are 4 bytes off retail: retail calls the
object hook as a member call — `mov ecx,esi / call eax` — and the draw dispatch
with `mov eax,esi / mov ecx,edi / call`, while the anchor uses `push`/`pop`
because this link has neither body visible). So it does **not** promote
`0x00448980`: that region needs `0x004451C0`'s body (216 bytes, itself an
assembly region) in the link and a member-call spelling of the hook. That is the
next dependency, and it is two call shapes, not a body-shape problem.

Independent corroboration: the parallel landing of `0x00448740` (task
`CamFreshPool`) carries its own `0x00448980` anchor in
`layout/ltcg/fun_00448740_wave11.json` and reproduced the same entry pair
(manager EDI, index EAX, EDI unpreserved) from a separately written body.

## Does this unblock anything?

Measured, not assumed:

* **No region flips as a direct consequence.** The five *other* callers of the
  landed `0x00421480` that wave 10 listed are `0x00439390` (with
  `CameraCallerA`), `0x00448620` (with `CameraCallerA`), `0x00448740` (now landed
  by `CamFreshPool` in its own cluster), and the two already handled here — my
  three regions were the remaining ones in `src/unidentified/`.
* **The `0x00448980` entry is now a solved, reusable lever.** Every member of the
  bucket family calls it exactly the way these three do, and until now no cluster
  had a body that earned its register pair. After this landing the camera cluster
  owns that anchor, so `0x00448620` (bucket 3, same `call 0x448980` tail, being
  finished by `CameraCallerA`) only needs its own body: it can declare
  `extern "C" int __cdecl asm_AnimationManager_RenderBucket_00448980(void*, int)`,
  call it, and merge into this span without adding a second anchor. The tiny
  wrappers `0x004485F0..0x00448740` and the game-thread callers at `0x00402B6D` /
  `0x00402CFB` / `0x00402D9C` need the same pair and can now be aimed at this
  anchor.
* **`roots` as a convention pin** is the transferable trick: a member function
  whose address escapes in retail (a scheduler task, a callback table) should be
  rooted rather than called from the synthetic context when its declared
  `__thiscall` receiver is the one retail uses. That is the cheaper spelling of
  "make the caller shape it" and it leaves the shared driver alone.
