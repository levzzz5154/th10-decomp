# ANM VM tick at 0x0043EE30: equivalent recovery, not byte-exact

The 9,964-byte region (9,588 bytes of code plus the 94-entry opcode table)
is registered under `equivalent_recoveries`. The default build compiles the
C++ below into the executable (9,948 of the region's 9,964 bytes, the rest
padding), after `tools/matching/equivalence.py` has checked it against the
exact bytes. The inline assembly in `msvc/src/script/anm_vm_tick.cpp` is kept
for the exact link that check compares against and for `make EXACT=1`.

## Files

- `msvc/equivalent/script/anm_vm_tick_0043ee30.cpp`: `AnmVm::Tick(AnmVm* vm)`.
- `msvc/equivalent/script/anm_vm_tick_0043ee30.hpp`: the VM, instruction,
  interpolator, and timer types.
- `msvc/equivalent/script/anm_vm_tick_0043ee30_context.cpp`: models of the
  callees and a caller, needed only so the checking `/GL` + `/LTCG` link
  derives the same private register conventions. Only `Tick` is compared.
- `msvc/layout/equivalent/anm_vm_tick_0043ee30.json`: symbol bindings and the
  recorded differences.

## How close it is

| Instructions | Count |
| --- | --- |
| Total | 2,755 |
| Identical | 2,709 (98.3%) |
| Recorded differences | 46, in 23 places |

Frame size (0xFC), prologue, register roles, the order of all opcode cases,
the jump table, and every call target and call convention match. What differs:

1. **Scratch register choice** (class `register-choice`, 23 instructions).
   Opcode 61 toggles the flip flag through `eax` in retail and `edx` in the
   model; opcodes 49, 53, and 54 load each variable argument through
   `edx`/`eax`/`ecx` in retail and `ecx`/`edx`/`eax` in the model; the vertex
   strip keeps its count in `esi` in retail and in `eax` with a copy in the
   model.
2. **Opcode 60** (class `evaluation-order`, 7 retail instructions). Retail
   keeps the duration in `esi` and loads the VM pointer before pushing; the
   model leaves it in `eax` and pushes first. Same arguments, same call.
3. **Rewind call** (class `evaluation-order`, 1 instruction). `lea esi` and
   `push -1.0f` are emitted in the other order before `FrameTimer::Advance`.
4. **Placement of the terminating cases** (class `block-order`, 11
   instructions). The block for opcodes -1, 1, and 2 (`return 1`) sits before
   the stop/rewind block in retail and after the first statement of the
   epilogue in the model. Both blocks end in a jump or return, and the checker
   follows branches and the jump table into the moved block.
5. **Loop alignment filler** (class `alignment-padding`) before the vertex
   strip loop, a consequence of the differences above.
6. **One sum** (class `operand-order`): `position.z + offset.z` in the second
   strip vertex.

No runtime test was performed; the equivalence claim rests on the instruction
comparison alone.

## What the source had to look like

- The interpolator evaluators (0x00404610, 0x00441600, 0x00441950,
  0x00441AD0) need real bodies in the context: their private conventions
  (`esi`; `eax` + `edi`; `eax` + stack; `esi` + `edi`) come from their own
  register use. They return by value. The colour one only takes its result
  pointer on the stack when `Int3::operator*(float)` is a separate function,
  as it is in retail (0x00441E50).
- `AnmVertex::SetPolar` (0x00441EF0) is an inline-assembly body inside the
  same link, not an external: retail keeps the angle in `edx` across both
  calls, which is only legal if the compiler saw that the callee preserves it.
- The colour temporaries in opcodes 57 and 78 are a three-byte struct whose
  constructor initialises `b`, `g`, `r` in member order. Assigning in any
  other order changes the shape of both opcodes and of 58, 59, and 79.
- `StartAlpha` (0x00442300) zeroes its control fields first; that is what puts
  three of its arguments on the stack.
- `Anm_FloatRef` takes `(vm, index, value, mask)`, `Anm_IntRef`
  `(value, vm, index, mask)`, with `index` an `int`.
- The strip origin is `offset + position` through an inlined `Float3`
  `operator+`; spelling the three sums out gives other operand orders.
- The main loop is a `while` over `(ins = vm->pc)->time <= vm->time.current`
  with the pending-interrupt entry jumping into opcode 63's body.

## Why exactness was not reached

- The placement of the `return 1` block did not respond to where the code is
  written: inside the switch, after the loop, or behind a `goto`. A `while`
  loop puts it after the epilogue's first statement; `for (;;)` and a
  hand-written `goto` loop put it at the end of the function. Retail has it in
  switch order.
- The scratch-register rotations are the same kind of state-dependent
  tie-break as the operand orders described in
  `ecl_core_interpreter_0044e1a0_2026_10_02.md`; no spelling of the affected
  statements moved them. They were not traced in the backend.
- Opcode 60 was tried with `StartScale` as a member and as a static with two
  parameter orders; none reproduces retail keeping the duration in `esi`.
