## Closest models

* `c_h2w1` (203 bytes): hit arm written `if (mode == 0 && F) 3; else if
  (mode != 0 && F) 0x26; else C`. The miss arm and the C block are retail's.
  Differences: the game pointer is loaded once above the hit/miss branch, the
  mode is tested twice and the flag byte is tested in memory.
* `e1` (211): local `unsigned char f` read before a single mode test. The hit
  arm has retail's instructions with other scratch registers (mode in EDX not
  EAX, flag in AL not CL); the miss arm degrades (immediate 1 in EAX, game
  pointer in ESI, flag in BL).

## What decides the rest (traced)

* Spillcosts: with a single mode test and no flag local (`e3`, `h1`, `h7`)
  the result variable (38) outranks `this` (36), takes ECX, and the private
  convention flips to player in EDX. Retail needs `this` above the result.
  `c_h2w1` has `this` at 42 because its hit block has K=5.
* Retail's `mov cl,[eax+0x378c]` sits between the mode `test` and its `jne`,
  after `mov eax,[ecx+0x44]` used EAX as scratch. Since CL overwrites `this`,
  the mode compare preceded the flag load when registers were assigned. That
  is what a late head merge of two arms that both start with
  `mov eax,G; <load flag>; test` would leave, or an optimiser hoist to the
  end of the mode block. Forms tried that do not produce it: flag bit-fields
  (byte and dword), an inline accessor, nested conditional expressions,
  `switch (mode)`, a kind local, default-then-override.
* `RegAssign` (c2 0x1079C134) gives an expression temporary its preferred
  register or, under /O2, the next free one in rotation; a register held by
  a global candidate is busy only in the blocks of that candidate's bit
  vector. In `t1` (e3 plus an artificial extra `this` reference to restore the
  convention) the two arms load the game pointer into EAX and ECX, so they
  are not identical and nothing is merged. In retail every temporary is EAX.

Open question: which source keeps ECX and EDX unavailable to temporaries in
the two hit arms (so both use EAX) while the flag test is still written per
arm.

## Exact-matching follow-up after equivalence work was stopped

User direction: prioritize byte-exact recovery; do not promote this candidate
through the equivalence gate. The production region remains assembly. The
experimental shorter-function verifier extension and its tests were removed
from the worktree and retained in `/tmp/codex-hit-equivalence-parked`.

normalized in the rig), not production byte-exact validation. The shared-flag
case does retain EAX as the mode scratch, but extends the flag lifetime across
both paths and therefore needs EBX. Keeping that lifetime confined to the hit
arm without losing EAX mode allocation remains the useful next question.
