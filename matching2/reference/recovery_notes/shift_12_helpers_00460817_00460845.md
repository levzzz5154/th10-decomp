# Three-word shift helpers at `0x00460817` and `0x00460845`

## Recovered behavior

Both helpers operate in place on three contiguous 32-bit unsigned words in little-endian significance order: word 0 at `+0`, word 1 at `+4`, and word 2 at `+8`.

- `___shl_12` at `0x00460817` (46 bytes) shifts the 96-bit value left by one. The old high bit of word 0 becomes the low bit of word 1; the old high bit of word 1 becomes the low bit of word 2. The old top bit of word 2 falls off.
- `___shr_12` at `0x00460845` (45 bytes) shifts the value right by one. The old low bit of word 2 becomes the top bit of word 1; the old low bit of word 1 becomes the top bit of word 0. The old low bit of word 0 falls off.

The C++ implementations snapshot all three original words before storing results. They use unsigned `int` shifts only, with a shift count of one or 31, so they do not depend on signed overflow or out-of-range shift behavior. The code assumes the target's 32-bit `unsigned int`, matching the x86 MSVC build.

Ghidra's listings and decompilation agree on the word order and carry direction. Both signatures are `void __cdecl helper(uint *words)`: the pointer is the sole stack argument, the helper modifies the pointed-to words, returns no value, and leaves argument cleanup to the caller.

Direct references to `___shr_12` are at `0x00460AE3` in `FUN_0046095A` and `0x00461A0B` in `FUN_00461841`.
