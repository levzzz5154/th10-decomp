# Scheduler callback allocator and redundant assembly cleanup

## Inline-asm-free callback allocator

`src/game/scheduler.cpp` now builds all three owned functions without inline
assembly. `SchedulerCallback_Create_00449ed0` remains 74 bytes and byte-exact.
Its two hand-written flag-load/mask/store blocks are replaced by a typed
`CallbackTask` constructor. A separate inline helper initializes the embedded
link, reproducing the same scheduling pattern as the FPS task factory recovery.

The allocation view names the full flag word rather than exposing only its
bitfields. The final volatile flag read preserves the existing observed load
before publishing the callback and setting the owned bit. All other fields
are ordinary typed C++. The allocation size is checked against SchedulerTask.
The function's external signature and allocation-failure behavior are preserved.

The probe validates all three complete functions:

```sh
python3 matching2/tools/matching/probe.py --source src/game/scheduler.cpp
```

## Redundant bodies removed

A source-tree audit found 107 naked assembly functions whose start addresses
already have authoritative semantic owners in other sources. Their non-owning
copies were removed. This includes old manager constructors/destructors and
unwind funclets now generated by native C++ compiler contexts. A total of 103
unused source files were removed along with their translation-unit entries.
The ownership mapping is recorded in `recovered_duplicate_removal_2026_10_04.json`.

Definitions were removed only when their address identifies a region owned by
another source and classified semantic or library. Files were deleted only when
they had no remaining definitions and no source/header/native-layout references.
Mixed sources keep their remaining authoritative code. The linker symbols used
by legacy assembly callers remain in the manifest.

The whole-image check passed after cleanup and after the callback conversion:
`make EXACT=1` reports a byte-identical 487,936-byte executable.

## Remaining inline assembly outside asm-classified regions

The audit also found inline x87 operations in four renderer source files that
are classified semantic: `projection_quads.cpp`, `quad_build_rotated.cpp`,
`sprite_quad_submit.cpp`, and `sprite_quad_submit_mirrored.cpp`. These still use
FSINCOS or FRNDINT and do not yet satisfy the inline-asm-free goal. The DOS
compatibility payload also contains assembly. Region classification alone is
therefore not sufficient evidence of complete C++ recovery.
