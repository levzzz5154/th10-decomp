## The residual is exactly one home, and retail's rule for it is now measured

Wave 13 left "the counter takes the first free register in canonical order, and
ESI precedes EDI — so the value must carry something that makes the backend
prefer EDI". Scanning all 18 retail sites of the `++manager->next_id` idiom
(load of `0x732454`, `inc`, store) with their full live sets sharpens that into
a rule with **12 verified instances**:

| site | node's register | tested copy | counter's load | free registers at the load |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0x004489D0 | ECX | ECX | ESI | {ESI, EDI} |
| 0x00448A50 | ECX | ECX | ESI | {ESI, EDI} |
| 0x00448AC0 | ECX | ECX | ESI | {ESI, EDI} |
| 0x00448B40 | ECX | ECX | ESI | {ESI, EDI} |
| 0x004094A1 | EAX | EAX | ECX | {ECX, EDX} |
| 0x004095F3 | EAX | EAX | EDX | {EDX} |
| 0x0040992C | EAX | EAX | EDX | {EDX} |
| 0x00413ECE | EAX | EAX | ECX | {ECX, EDX} |
| 0x00414009 | EAX | EAX | EDX | {EDX} |
| 0x004142BD | EAX | EAX | EDX | {EDX} |
| 0x00414EFF | EAX | EAX | ECX | {ECX, EDX} |
| 0x0041E3A8 | EAX | EAX | ECX | {ECX, EDX} |

Two rules hold in **12 of 12** sites:

1. the register that holds the *tested* copy is the register the **node pointer**
   has just been freed from, and
2. the register the counter is loaded into is the **lowest-numbered free
   register excluding that copy register** (EAX < ECX < EDX < EBX < ESI < EDI in
   c2's encoding order): ESI over EDI in the four leaves, ECX over EDX at
   0x4094A1/0x413ECE/0x414EFF/0x41E3A8, EDX alone where it is the only one free.

Our model obeys rule 1 (`mov %edi,%ecx`) and is the **single violation of rule 2
anywhere in the image**: with the pool {ESI, EDI} it takes EDI. So the residual
is not "the backend prefers EDI" but "**the backend thinks ESI is occupied at
the counter's definition**". The only value that can occupy ESI there is the
`tail` pointer, whose last use is the else block's last instruction.

## The schedule and the home are separable, and the home is the real blocker

Wave 13 separated the reorder (the tail store landing after the counter load)
from the home. Wave 14 shows the *converse*: forcing retail's order does **not**
move the home. An MSVC ordering barrier between the tail store and the id block

```c
    *(void**)(m + 0x72DAD8) = node;
    _ReadWriteBarrier();                        /* declared extern "C" */
    int id = ++*(int*)(m + 0x732454);
```

emits the store **before** the load and the then-arm's `jmp` targeting the
store, i.e. retail's block structure exactly — and still 41 differing bytes with
the counter in **EDI**:

```text
40: 89 71 08  mov %esi,0x8(%ecx)
43: 89 8a d8 da 72 00  mov %ecx,0x72dad8(%edx)   <- retail's position
49: 8b ba 54 24 73 00  mov 0x732454(%edx),%edi   <- retail has %esi
4f: 47  inc %edi
50: 8b cf  mov %edi,%ecx
52: 85 c9  test %ecx,%ecx
54: 89 ba 54 24 73 00  mov %edi,0x732454(%edx)
5a: 5f 5e  pop %edi / pop %esi
```

(The barrier is a *probe*, not a candidate: it is not part of any recovered
idiom, and it does not close the region anyway.) The mirror probe — the folded
`Q` shape, whose counter lands in ECX and therefore **forces** the store first
through a write-after-read on that register — reaches retail's order with a
single register and the folded `movl $0x1` zero case (117 bytes). So:

* order (store before load) is reachable: by a WAR on the counter's register
  (Q) or by an ordering barrier (probe);
* the home is **not** moved by the order: it is an independent allocator
  decision, and it is the one that is wrong.

## Can the 0x448D50 anchor be dropped?

No. Measured again on the current tree: with the anchor record replaced by an
`absolute_symbols` `relative_code` binding at 0x004489D0 (so the body leaves the
link) and the driver's body replaced by a declaration, the build reports

```text
FUNCTION SIZE MOVED .text$05accessor 95 -> 99
BUILD ERR: native cluster image base or entry address differs
```

i.e. the pass re-decides the caller and its contribution grows from 95 to 99
bytes (wave 12's absolute binding cost 78 of 95 bytes with a slightly different
setup). The anchor is load-bearing as long as the body is not exact. Wave 13's
measurement still stands for the *other* direction: declaring the region as a
selected function of the 0x448D50 cluster instead of an anchor emits the very
same body, so that switch costs nothing once the home is right.

## Where the region stands

* **Not EXACT**, nothing registered, nothing unblocked. The four-leaf family
  (0x00448A50 / 0x00448AC0 / 0x00448B40) shares the blocker exactly, and
  `0x00448D50` keeps its 103-byte stand-in anchor and probes EXACT 95 with
  `differing=0` in every run above.
* The two best candidates are unchanged: **P** (118 bytes, 40 differing, EDI)
  and **Q** (117, 39 differing, ECX), source text in
  `004489d0_wave13.md` and in `/tmp/RegisterLeaf/bodies/{P,Q}.c`.
* What is left is a single allocator decision, stated exactly: the counter must
  be the **lowest-numbered free register at its definition** (rule 2 above), and
  the model's ESI is occupied there. The measurements above rule out the
  schedule (forced both ways), the parameter order, the body's receiver-ness and
  a co-compiled twin of the idiom as levers. What has *not* been tried: giving
  the *counter's value* a home of its own by making it live in a construct the
  backend cannot split (a loop or a call around the id block), and a build with
  the other three leaves of the family in the link *as called* functions rather
  than an uncalled twin.
